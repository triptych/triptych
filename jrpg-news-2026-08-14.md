## Persona 4 Revival — New Features Trailer Highlights Send Flying, Full Voice, and New Music

**Atlus** released a new two-minute trailer on **August 11, 2026** walking through five gameplay and production additions exclusive to **Persona 4 Revival**, narrated by **Paul Castro Jr.** (the English voice of Yosuke Hanamura).

The five features highlighted:

- **Fully Voiced Events**: All Social Link and story events are newly recorded with full voice acting in both English and Japanese. The original game was mostly silent outside of battle and a handful of key scenes; Revival extends performance to daily-life interactions throughout.
- **Send Flying**: A new battle mechanic that triggers when a normal attack lands on an enemy with a status ailment. The shadow gets knocked back; if it collides with a second enemy, the collision deals additional damage and spreads the ailment to that target.
- **New Music**: **Ryota Kozuka** (composer on *Persona Q2* and *Persona 5 Royal* additional tracks) contributed entirely new compositions alongside remixed arrangements of classic *Persona 4* tracks.
- **Baton Pass**: Carried forward from *Persona 5 Royal*, the mechanic lets players pass a boosted turn to a party member who already used their action, extending momentum across the team after landing a weakness or critical.
- **Prime Time Meter**: A tension gauge introduced during the AX 2026 showcase; the trailer confirms it builds across consecutive combos and ailment spreads, unlocking powerful team follow-up actions when filled.

The game still launches **February 18, 2027** on **PC via Steam** (also PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S with Xbox Game Pass).

## Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave — August 4 Direct Reveals True Protagonist; Preview Roundup Lands August 13

**Nintendo** broadcast a roughly 20-minute dedicated **Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Direct** on **August 4** that substantially expanded the picture beyond the four-protagonist reveal covered earlier this year.

**True Protagonist — Eshmel**

The biggest reveal is that Fortune's Weave has a fifth, fully customizable protagonist. Their default name is **Eshmel**, and players can set name, body type, skin tone, hairstyle, hair color, and outfit. Eshmel is voiced in English by **Virginia Lee** and **Joshua Waters** and in Japanese by **Maaya Sakamoto** and **Yoshitsugu Matsuoka**. Eshmel is not one of the Heroic Games competitors; rather, they are summoned by the progenitor god **Sothis** from a grim timeline five years in the future, after the resurgent demon god **Balor** has already destroyed the Dagdan Empire and scattered the four champions.

**Time-Travel Loop Structure**

Using the time-manipulating power of the goddess **Fortuna**, Eshmel travels back to the Heroic Games period and works across all four champions' storylines simultaneously. Players can hop between any of the four heroes at will, with each thread influencing the others. The setup explicitly echoes *Fire Emblem: Three Houses'* parallel-house structure while folding in a sci-fi-adjacent framing where magic can be channeled through firearms in the future timeline's segments.

**Returning Mechanics**

The Direct confirmed **dungeon crawling** in the vein of *Fire Emblem Gaiden / Echoes: Shadows of Valentia*, applied to the Heroic Games venue's underground areas. **Blaze Arts** let the four champions unleash high-damage special abilities that build off the new **Fortuna Gauge**. The game is described as the series' most accessible to date for difficulty customization.

**August 13 Preview Roundup**

Media embargo lifted on **August 13**, with hands-on impressions from My Nintendo News, Game Informer, Kotaku, Serenes Forest, The Gamer, TheSixthAxis, Press Start, and Console Creatures going live simultaneously. The consensus: Fortune's Weave is extremely ambitious in scope — multiple outlets called it the largest-feeling Fire Emblem since Three Houses — with some reviewers noting the early chapters take time to reveal the game's full structure.

**Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave** launches exclusively on **Nintendo Switch 2** on **September 17, 2026**.

## Suikoden STAR LEAP Launches in Japan — First New Series Entry in 14 Years

**Konami** and developer **MYTHRIL** launched **Suikoden STAR LEAP** on **iOS and Android in Japan on August 7, 2026**, making it the first all-new title in the *Suikoden* series since 2012.

The game is set in **SY 453**, a few years before the events of the original *Suikoden I*, and features an original cast while drawing on the franchise's signature political intrigue and 108 Stars of Destiny recruitment framework. A pre-launch live stream, **Suikoden Live Vol. 7: Suikoden STAR LEAP Pre-Launch Program**, aired on August 6.

## Final Fantasy XIV on Switch 2 — Reviews Land; Port Earns Cautious Praise

**Final Fantasy XIV Online** launched on **Nintendo Switch 2 on August 4**, and reviews and impressions have been accumulating since. The consensus is broadly positive with a few noted caveats.

**What works**: Reviewers at 80.lv, Gaming Trend, and RPGFan describe the port as better than expected — the full breadth of content including all expansions through **Dawntrail** is present and playable, and the hybrid format genuinely suits an MMO designed around steady daily routines. Load times are considered acceptable. Mouse-and-keyboard support via USB-C is functional and welcome.

**What doesn't**: RPG Site's technical breakdown notes that docked performance falls short of what the hardware should theoretically deliver — resolution and shadow quality in docked mode look noticeably softer than expected, while handheld mode comes off more favorably by comparison. Nintendo Life's review landed today (August 14), calling it "an expansive port that demands your commitment" and recommending it for players who haven't experienced the game on another platform, while noting veterans are unlikely to migrate their primary client.

**Subscription model**: Switch 2 players do not need Nintendo Switch Online; they subscribe directly through Square Enix at the same monthly rate as other platforms, minus a discount of approximately 50% for players already subscribed elsewhere. Square Enix confirmed approximately one free month for new Switch 2 subscribers at launch to ease server onboarding.

## Onimusha: Way of the Sword — Hands-On Preview Roundup Ahead of September 4 Launch

A final wave of **Onimusha: Way of the Sword** hands-on previews published on **August 6**, giving the most complete pre-launch picture yet of Capcom's first new *Onimusha* entry in over 20 years.

The game is set in **Kyoto at the dawn of the Edo period**, now overrun by the demonic **Genma** and shrouded in an eerie Malice fog. Streets are described as stunningly detailed — corpses in the road, buildings in ruin, unnerving quiet broken only by the clash of steel. The protagonist wields the Oni Gauntlet and a customizable arsenal of period weapons.

Preview coverage from **Nintendo Life**, **Shacknews**, **Worthplaying**, and **PlayStation Blog** all note that the game starts tight and linear before opening into broader, more exploration-friendly mission areas. The combat system — parry-heavy, with enemy-specific counters tied to demon ability absorption — was highlighted as the strongest aspect. The Switch 2 version was playable at the preview event and noted as technically solid.

**Onimusha: Way of the Sword** launches **September 4, 2026** on **Nintendo Switch 2, PC via Steam** (also PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series).

## Brigandine: Abyss — August 26–27 Release Set; Previews Highlight Rally Mechanic and Six-Campaign Scope

**Matrix Corporation** and **Happinet Games** are bringing **Brigandine: Abyss** to **PC via Steam on August 26** and **Nintendo Switch 2** on **August 27** (also PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series). A physical Deluxe Edition for Switch 2 and PS5 in North America and Europe includes a mini art book and digital soundtrack.

The game is set on **Meltitea**, a new continent where the ancient Abyssola Empire was sealed away by the power of an arcane Brigandine — but a new imperial faction has risen and seeks conquest. Players choose from **six story campaigns**, each following a different faction with its own cutscenes and Event Battles. A separate **Mission Mode** unlocks all **24 factions** individually, each with unique win conditions, giving the game exceptional replay depth.

Combat operates across three phases — Organization (territory management, army equipping), Attack (orders and positioning), and Invasion (hexagonal grid battles) — continuing the series' hybrid war-simulation/SRPG formula.

The notable new system is **Rally**: monsters can forfeit their independent actions to accompany their knight leader, transferring their Rally Traits as passive effects to the whole squad. Traits include knockback properties, elemental damage additions, defensive boosts, and action bonuses, creating meaningful synergy decisions during army composition.

## Expeditions: Samurai Enters Early Access — Feudal Japan Turn-Based RPG From THQ Nordic Showcase

**Campfire Cabal** (founded by veterans of series developer Logic Artists) launched **Expeditions: Samurai** into **Steam Early Access on August 7**, the same day as the **THQ Nordic Digital Showcase 2026** where it was featured.

The game is a historical turn-based RPG set in **Japan circa 1600**, in a timeline where navigator **William Adams** never becomes Japan's first European samurai — because the player character arrives first. Players lead a crew of Dutch privateers aboard the frigate *De Albatros* and are drawn into a civil war whose outcome depends on their choices. The narrative is explicitly nonlinear, with the studio noting that even minor decisions have compounding consequences for the crew and Japan's future.

Core mechanics include **tactical turn-based combat**, alongside stealth and diplomacy as full-featured alternatives to fighting. The full PC release is planned for **2027**.

## Metaphor: ReFantazio Switch 2 — Preview Raises Performance Questions Before November Launch

**Atlus** and **SEGA** announced **Metaphor: ReFantazio** for **Nintendo Switch 2 on November 12** back in June, and a preview build circulated in July at Anime Expo. The RPG Site preview — published this week — offers the most technical look yet at the port.

Targeting **60 FPS**, the Switch 2 build runs noticeably better than feared: framerate holds close to 60 in most areas, with the clearest dips occurring inside dungeons. The preview only covered **docked mode** (handheld was not available), leaving that mode's performance unassessed ahead of launch. Visually the game holds up reasonably well compared to the PC and PS5 releases, though some texture and shadow detail is reduced.

The preview's conclusion: the port is "frustratingly close" to a clean recommendation. Atlus has roughly three months before launch to tighten framerate and improve image quality. The game is priced significantly below the original release — approximately ¥2,990 in Japan, a roughly 35% discount versus the 2024 launch price — reflecting its position as a back-catalogue port for players who skipped it on other platforms.

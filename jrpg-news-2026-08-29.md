## Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Direct — Full Mechanics and True Protagonist Revealed

Nintendo aired a dedicated **Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Direct** on **August 4, 2026**, running approximately 20 minutes and providing the deepest look yet at the final game ahead of its **September 17 launch exclusively on Nintendo Switch 2**.

The most striking revelation concerns the game's narrative structure. Fortune's Weave is set on the continent of **Dagda**, far to the southwest of Fire Emblem: Three Houses's Fódlan. Five years in the future, the revived demon god **Balor** has destroyed the world. The progenitor goddess **Sothis** sends back a **nameless hero clad in white** — the game's true protagonist, controlled by the player — to change history by influencing the fates of four champions before the apocalypse arrives.

Those four champions — **Cai, Dietrich, Theodora, and Leda** — serve as playable secondary protagonists. Players can switch between any of them at will, with each character's story progress saved independently. The design lets players experience four interweaving narratives in parallel or one at a time.

Mechanically, the game features the series' traditional grid-based SRPG combat for arena and overworld battles. Weapons include swords, axes, spears, bows, gauntlets, and magic, each with their own feel; **Combat Arts** deal bonus damage at the cost of weapon durability, and **Blaze Arts** deliver powerful signature moves at the cost of HP. A notable wrinkle: dungeon sequences strip away the grid entirely and replace it with **traditional turn-based party combat** in the style of classic Final Fantasy or Dragon Quest.

## Final Fantasy XIV Launches on Nintendo Switch 2 — Launch Details and Early Impressions

**Final Fantasy XIV** launched on **Nintendo Switch 2 on August 4, 2026** at 3:00 a.m. PDT, fulfilling the announcement made at the Berlin Fan Festival. The Switch 2 version targets **30 frames per second** — half the framerate of the PS5 and Xbox Series versions — and requires a **117.2 GB** installation. Early streams showed the game running well within that target.

The Switch 2 version requires a **separate FFXIV subscription** and does not share a subscription with other platforms. Existing subscribers on PC or PS5 receive a **50% discount** on the Switch 2 sub. To ease onboarding, Square Enix waived subscription fees for approximately **one free month** following launch; the standard 30-day free period for new platform registrations begins after that window closes.

Edition pricing: Starter Edition **$19.99**, Complete Edition **$59.99** (pre-order price $41.99), Complete Collector's Edition **$139.99**. Both Complete Edition variants were **30% off through August 13** to celebrate launch. The free trial — covering A Realm Reborn through the end of Heavensward with no level cap — was also made available on Switch 2 on launch day.

The Switch 2 version supports **mouse-and-keyboard input** via the USB-C port and does not require a Nintendo Switch Online subscription.

## Xenoblade Chronicles 2 — Switch 2 Edition Reviews: Near-Universal Acclaim

**Xenoblade Chronicles 2 – Nintendo Switch 2 Edition** launched digitally on **July 30** and has now accumulated a wave of full reviews, with critics landing firmly in the positive-to-excellent range.

The Switch 2 edition runs the original game at **4K resolution docked / 1080p handheld** with a **stable 60 FPS** in both modes — a massive leap from the original Switch version, which struggled to maintain 30 FPS and ran at significantly lower internal resolution. The upgrades are supplemented by the exclusive **Momo Rare Blade** (a crossover character from Xenosaga), new **Pyra and Mythra outfits** by character designer Masatsugu Saito, and an expanded **Merc Assault** system supporting up to six deployed Blades per mission.

**Selected review scores:**
- **CGMagazine** — 9/10: "The visual improvements are so significant it almost feels like a full remaster."
- **TheSixthAxis** — Called it "the ultimate form of this classic JRPG."
- **RPGFan** — "A worthwhile upgrade" in their hands-on impressions.
- **Digitally Downloaded** — Positive, noting the 60 FPS transformation elevates the experience substantially.

The physical edition is still on track for **October 1**.

## Blue Reflection Quartet — Launch Reviews: Mixed Reception

**Blue Reflection Quartet** — the compilation collecting *Blue Reflection*, *Blue Reflection: Second Light*, *Blue Reflection: Ray*, and *Blue Reflection: Sun* — launched on **July 30** for Switch 2, Switch, and PC (also PS5). Review coverage has now fully landed and the reception is notably mixed.

**Selected assessments:**
- **Game8** — **68/100**: "Half-baked collection that fails to elevate the series further… Ray and Sun adaptations are extremely lackluster."
- **Final Weapon** — **4/5**: "A magical girl JRPG collection focused on friendship, personal growth, and heartfelt storytelling; phenomenal audio design elevates the experience."
- **RPG Site**: "Tremendous value and a great way to start the series, but some parts of the collection leave a lot to be desired."
- **Capsule Computers**: "New visuals are still quite dated and unimpressive from a performance standpoint, especially on Switch 2."
- **Anime News Network**: "Second Light steals the show; the remake of Sun is a nice addition."

The consensus places it as a worthwhile entry point for the curious, with *Second Light* widely singled out as the standout title of the package.

## Brigandine: Abyss Launches — New Entry in Classic SRPG Series

**Brigandine: Abyss** launched **August 26 on PC via Steam** and **August 27 on Nintendo Switch 2** (also PS5 and Xbox). Developed by Matrix Software (known for several classic JRPG ports) and published by **Happinet**, it is the newest mainline entry in the Brigandine grand strategy RPG series.

The game is set in a **self-contained new world** unconnected to prior entries, where the Abyssloa Empire was halted centuries ago by the power of the arcane Brigandine — but a new empire has arisen seeking global conquest. Players choose from **six story campaigns**, each following a different faction with its own perspective on the war.

A separate **Mission Mode** puts players in command of any of **24 distinct factions**, each with unique win conditions, providing a replayable sandbox beyond the story campaigns. The physical **Deluxe Edition** — available for PS5 and Switch 2 in North America and Europe — includes a mini art book and digital soundtrack.

## Neptunia Unlimited Launches in Japan

**Hyperdimension Neptunia Unlimited** — the first new mainline entry in the *Neptunia* series in over a decade — launched in Japan on **August 27, 2026** for **PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4 (digital only), Nintendo Switch 2, and Nintendo Switch** at ¥8,580 (Limited Edition: ¥19,800, including art book, soundtrack CD, Season Pass, and a Tsunako-illustrated special box).

The game marks a significant gameplay departure: rather than the series' traditional turn-based combat, Unlimited uses an **action RPG battle system** with comparisons to the *Tales* series. New characters are introduced alongside the returning CPU goddesses.

A Western release date has not yet been announced.

## Dungeon Antiqua 2 Launches on Switch — Sequel to NES-Inspired Dungeon Crawler

**Dungeon Antiqua 2**, the sequel to **AMATA K.K.**'s retro-style dungeon crawler, launched on **Nintendo Switch** (also PS5 and Xbox Series) on **August 20, 2026**, priced at **$10.99**.

Where the original Dungeon Antiqua drew from NES-era RPGs, the sequel adopts a **SNES-style pixel art and sound** aesthetic. It blends strategic exploration with a **highly flexible character-building system** and 2D dungeon hack-and-slash combat. Like the first game, the title supports English, Japanese, and Simplified Chinese.

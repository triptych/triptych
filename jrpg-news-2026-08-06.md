## Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Direct — Battle System, Time Jump, and Manga Adaptation Revealed

Nintendo held a dedicated **Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Direct** on **August 4**, running approximately 22 minutes and substantially expanding what is known about the tactical RPG's mechanics.

New details confirmed by the Direct:

- **Blaze Arts**: Each of the four protagonists — **Cai, Dietrich, Leda, and Theodora** — has a unique special ability that charges during battle and can be unleashed for massive, fight-turning effects. Each Blaze Art is visually and mechanically distinct.
- **Time jump**: Like *Three Houses*, *Fortune's Weave* features a **five-year time skip** that alters characters' appearances and the political landscape of the continent of **Dagda**.
- **Exploration outside the city**: Players can use action-point turns to move across a regional map, discovering dungeons and skirmish locations beyond the main hub. Enemy difficulty scales to the route being taken.
- **All four stories in a single playthrough**: Unlike *Three Houses*, which required separate playthroughs per route, *Fortune's Weave* lets players experience all four protagonists' perspectives within one continuous campaign, with the narrative branching and reuniting as the Heroic Games progress.

On the merchandise side, Nintendo and **Shueisha** announced a **manga adaptation** of *Fortune's Weave*, with the prologue chapter appearing in the **October 2026 issue of Saikyo Jump**, due on newsstands in September.

*Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave* launches on **Nintendo Switch 2 on September 17, 2026**.

## Beast of Reincarnation Launches to Mixed Critical Reception — Game Freak's First Major Non-Pokémon RPG in Years

**Game Freak**'s **Beast of Reincarnation** launched on **August 4** for **PC (Steam)**, PlayStation 5, and Xbox. It is the developer's first multiplatform AAA action RPG and their highest-profile non-Pokémon release in decades.

Set in **Japan in the year 4026** — a post-apocalyptic landscape reclaimed by corruption — the game follows **Emma the Sealer** and her dog **Koo** across a one-person, one-dog action RPG. Combat fuses real-time swordplay with a companion-tactics system: Emma parries and attacks in real time, generating skill points to spend on Koo's specialized abilities through a menu that briefly slows the action. Emma also uses plant-like hair vines for traversal, scaling ruined structures and striking from elevation.

**Critical reception landed mixed.** The game holds a **Metacritic score of 73 on PS5 / 72 on PC**, with roughly half of critics positive and half mixed — no negative reviews as of this writing. GamesRadar reports that player reaction on Steam has been similarly divided, with debate centering on whether the combat is "slick" or "uninspired." The consensus across the bulk of reviews: strong core action undermined by repetitive encounter design and an underdeveloped story.

## Final Fantasy XIV Launches on Nintendo Switch 2

**Final Fantasy XIV** officially went live on **Nintendo Switch 2 on August 4** at 3:00 a.m. PDT, completing a long-anticipated platform expansion. The Switch 2 version includes the base game (*A Realm Reborn*) and all expansions through *Dawntrail*.

Subscription fees are waived for approximately **one month following launch** — new Switch 2 players then begin the 30-day free period included with all new platform registrations before standard subscription costs apply. A **Nintendo Switch Online subscription is not required** to play. The Switch 2 version supports **mouse-and-keyboard input via USB-C** and has full crossplay and shared character progression with the PC and PlayStation versions.

Nintendo Life published an extended gameplay showcase video this week walking through the Switch 2 interface and performance. Editions are priced from **$19.99 to $97.99**, with both Complete Edition tiers discounted 30% until August 13.

## Pokémon Pokopia: Bubbly Basin Expansion Launches, Adds Underwater Exploration

**The Pokémon Company** released **Part 1 of the Pokémon Pokopia Expansion Pass — "Bubbly Basin"** on **August 5** for Nintendo Switch 2.

*Bubbly Basin* introduces a new coastal town in need of restoration, where a lone **Popplio** asks the player to help revive it. Players construct new habitats to attract Water-type Pokémon to assist in rebuilding. The headline new mechanic is **underwater exploration**: after learning the move **Dive** from **Manaphy** (available via the free Ver. 2.0.0 update for all owners), players can swim alongside Water-type Pokémon and construct buildings and habitats beneath the surface — a first for the franchise.

The Ver. 2.0.0 **free update** launched simultaneously and is available to all Pokopia owners regardless of whether they purchase the Expansion Pass. The Expansion Pass is priced at **$34.99** and covers all three planned parts; **Part 2** is planned for "late 2026" and **Part 3** for 2027.

## Fields of Mistria 1.0 Out Now — Farm-Life RPG Leaves Early Access

**NPC Studio**'s **Fields of Mistria** left Steam Early Access with its full **Version 1.0 release on August 5**, more than two years after its initial Early Access debut.

The 1.0 update is substantial: it completes the **main storyline**, adds the **marriage and children** systems, and introduces a range of new activities, items, and late-game content. *Mistria* is a fantasy-adjacent farm and life sim RPG set in the magical town of Mistria, drawing comparisons to *Stardew Valley* in structure while leaning into a heavier JRPG-style narrative and character roster.

## Blue Reflection Quartet Reviews: Mixed but Recommended for Series Newcomers

**Blue Reflection Quartet** — the four-in-one collection of the original *BLUE REFLECTION*, *BLUE REFLECTION: Second Light*, the *BLUE REFLECTION RAY* anime, and the revamped *BLUE REFLECTION SUN* mobile game — launched on **July 30** for Nintendo Switch 2, Switch, and PC (priced at $49.99). Reviews have now come in across outlets.

The **Metacritic score** for the PS5 version has settled in the **mid-to-high 60s**, with roughly similar results across Switch 2 and PC. The critical split is consistent: *Second Light* and the original *BLUE REFLECTION* are praised for their emotionally resonant storytelling around themes of friendship, anxiety, and identity, while the *RAY* anime integration and the *SUN* adaptation are widely criticized as undercooked. The collection is broadly recommended as a **series entry point** given its value — over 100 hours of content — but criticized as a missed opportunity to properly celebrate the franchise.

Notable reviews: RPG Site gave it a positive write-up, Noisy Pixel called it "a hollow compilation that fails the series legacy," and Anime News Network offered a measured positive take focused on the two mainline games.

## Disgaea Mayhem Reviews: Fun Combat, Thin Campaign

**Disgaea Mayhem** (launched July 23) has completed its review cycle. The action RPG spinoff — a significant genre departure from the series' trademark strategy-grid roots — holds a **Metacritic score of 60 (PS5) / 57 (Switch 2)** and a **64 on OpenCritic**, with only **26% of critics recommending it**.

The critical consensus across outlets is remarkably consistent: the real-time combat engine is genuinely enjoyable, but the campaign runs **under 10 hours** and is built around repetitive stage design. Nintendo Life scored it 5/10; GamingBolt scored it 7. The game is available on **Nintendo Switch 2, Switch, and PC via Steam** (also PS5) at $59.99.

## EXSTETRA HD Remaster on Steam Now; Console Release Set for September 3

**Marvelous** released the **EXSTETRA HD Remaster** on **Steam on July 30** — the first time the 2013 Japan-only PS Vita/3DS RPG has been available in English. Console versions for **PlayStation 5, Nintendo Switch, and Nintendo Switch 2** are confirmed for **September 3, 2026**, priced at $29.99.

*EXSTETRA* is a fantasy JRPG set in **Amazea**, a dimension formed when fragments of modern Tokyo merged with another world. Protagonist **Ryoma** becomes a **Prisma** — a chosen figure who can absorb **EXS energy** — and the game's unusual central mechanic: kisses transfer EXS power and are mechanically central to progression. The HD Remaster adds English localization (the original never left Japan), improved resolution, and quality-of-life updates.

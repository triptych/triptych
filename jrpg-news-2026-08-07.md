## Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Direct — September 17 Confirmed, Class System and Protagonist Stories Detailed

Nintendo held a **Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Direct** on **August 4, 2026**, running approximately **20 minutes** and packed with new story, character, and systems information for the upcoming Switch 2 tactical RPG.

**Release date confirmed:** September 17, 2026 on Nintendo Switch 2.

The Direct provided the most complete look yet at the four protagonists competing in the **Heroic Games**, a gladiator-style tournament through which each seeks to have a wish granted:

- **Cai** — A young man from the peaceful village of Ribeira who enters the tournament to free his unjustly imprisoned father.
- **Theodora** — A royal from the kingdom of Saramis who fights to restore the honor of the Keeper of Death and Night, her kingdom's reviled goddess, and preserve her homeland's peace.
- **Leda** — Details were expanded on her role as an idealistic rebel trying to topple the tournament's corrupt ruling structure from within.
- **Dietrich** — A veteran soldier entering as a mercenary, whose motivations reveal darker layers tied to the tournament's origins.

The Direct confirmed players can switch between protagonists at any time, with each character's story progress saved independently — allowing all four narratives to be experienced in parallel rather than sequentially.

On the systems side, **classes** now function as modular loadouts rather than permanent assignments. Each character can equip a primary class for stats and combat role while freely drawing skills from any class they have previously mastered, rewarding experimentation without punishing commitment. The **free-time structure** for recruiting, training, and bonding with units returns as a franchise staple, including support conversations and recruitable allies within each protagonist's arc.

## Beast of Reincarnation Launches — Game Freak's First AAA Action RPG Receives Mixed Reviews

**Game Freak** released **Beast of Reincarnation** on **August 4, 2026** for PC via Steam, PlayStation 5, and Xbox Series X|S — the studio's first major non-Pokémon title and their most ambitious project in years.

Set in a **post-apocalyptic Japan of 4026**, the game follows warrior **Emma** and her dog **Koo** as they traverse a landscape reclaimed by enormous, corrupted beasts. Combat fuses fast-paced real-time swordplay for Emma, drawing comparisons to *Sekiro*, with command-based tactical abilities for Koo — the "one-person, one-dog" system at the game's core. The director is **Kota Furushima**, a veteran of multiple Pokémon battle and sound teams.

Critical reception has been **mixed to positive**. Famitsu awarded it a **35/40**, calling it a confident debut for Furushima's team. The game holds a **73 on Metacritic** and lands in the **63rd percentile on OpenCritic**. IGN issued a 6/10, citing the game's world as beautiful but its traversal as repetitive. Other critics were warmer: *WccfTech* called it "an intoxicating mixture of Sekiro, NieR, and Pokémon," and *Final Weapon* praised the atmospheric world and traversal while noting story and technical inconsistencies. Nintendo Life's roundup characterized it as "a mixed bag" overall.

The game is included in **Xbox Game Pass** for PC on day one.

## Blue Reflection Quartet Reviews In — Collection Earns Warm Reception

**Koei Tecmo** and **Gust**'s **Blue Reflection Quartet** launched on **July 30, 2026**, and reviews have now settled into a generally positive picture. The collection gathers the entire *Blue Reflection* franchise — the original game, *Second Light*, the *Ray* anime adaptation content, and the mobile game *Sun* — at a budget price on PS5, Switch 2, Switch, and PC.

Metacritic placed it at **79** based on early scores. Highlights from critics:

- **GameSpew** called it "a no-brainer for fans and newcomers alike," praising the value proposition and the way *Second Light* in particular holds up as a standout magical-girl RPG.
- **The Outerhaven** noted it "fits four experiences into one collection" effectively, with the two main games being the clear draws.
- **Shacknews** (7/10) appreciated the two main games while observing that the *Ray* anime adaptation is "weird" and the reconstructed *Sun* mobile content doesn't fully recover from its gacha origins.

The consensus: the two standalone RPGs make the collection worth it for series newcomers, while existing fans may find limited new incentive beyond convenience.

## Nioh 3: Hell Rising DLC Set for August 19 — New Weapons, Story, and Difficulty

**Koei Tecmo** and **Team Ninja** confirmed that **Hell Rising**, the first DLC expansion for **Nioh 3**, will arrive on **August 19, 2026** for PlayStation 5 and PC via Steam.

The expansion takes protagonist **Takechiyo** to **1651 Edo during the Keian era**, a region consumed by the Crucible. New allies include one-eyed swordsman **Yagyu Jubei** and Spanish envoy **Rodrigo**. Hell Rising adds:

- **Hoko** and **shield** weapon types — the first time shields appear as a dedicated weapon category in the series
- **"Enlightened One's Journey"** — a new post-game difficulty tier beyond the base game's hardest setting
- **Hundred Demon Realms Picture Scroll** — a new rare equipment category with unique drop mechanics
- Customizable challenge battles and an expanded skill tree

The DLC is included in the **$39.99 Season Pass** and the **$109.99 Digital Deluxe Edition**.

## Beaten Path Announced — FFT-Inspired Tactical RPG Coming to Switch and PC in 2027

Publisher **Wales Interactive** and developer **Peacebreak Studio** announced **Beaten Path**, a turn-based tactical RPG targeting **2027** on **PlayStation 5, Nintendo Switch, and PC** via Steam and the Epic Games Store.

The game follows **Panna** and up to **25 recruitable companions** as they confront a world ruled by fallen gods and ancient machines, with a story built around time loops, lost memories, and mythic powers. Grid-based combat draws direct inspiration from **Final Fantasy Tactics** and **Fire Emblem**, with a full job system allowing each character to change class and inherit skills across roles.

Beaten Path was originally funded on Kickstarter in 2025, reaching its target within six days, and has been in development for nearly five years. Wales Interactive will handle publishing.
